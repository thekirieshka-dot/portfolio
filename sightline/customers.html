Four teams. Four ugly questions.

Each of these teams was waiting on a number. Here is what they asked, what they connected, and how the wait changed. The charts are theirs; scroll and watch the line cross over.

(01)Research software · Utrecht · 140 people

Fieldnote

The analyst queue went from 41 tickets a week to six.

Fieldnote / Analyst tickets per weekBeforeAfter

Source: Fieldnote ticket tracker, analytics queue. Sightline connected in week 31.

01The question

Nobody could ask a question without filing one.

Every Thursday the product managers at Fieldnote sent the data team a list. How many teams used offline sync last week? Did the new import flow change activation? Three analysts spent about two days of every week answering questions that took a sentence to ask.

02What they connected

Eleven minutes to connect, an afternoon to agree.

A Postgres replica, a BigQuery events warehouse and billing. Setup took eleven minutes; the semantic layer took an afternoon. The analysts defined “active team” once, and found out they had been using three definitions.

03What changed

Fifty tickets became one definition.

PMs asked 1,900 questions in the first month. Most never reached an analyst. Some came back as a request to define a metric properly, which the analysts did, once, in the semantic layer, instead of fifty times in fifty tickets.

04The number

Six tickets, and a model that shipped.

Six tickets a week, all of them genuinely hard. And the attribution model the team had postponed since spring shipped in week 36.

(02)Freight marketplace · Rotterdam · 310 people

Pallet

Median time to an answer fell from 2.8 days to six minutes.

Pallet / Hours until a question was answeredBeforeAfter

Source: Pallet request log, 30 questions per period. Sightline connected in week 14.

01The question

Three numbers for one delivery.

Pallet matches shippers with empty trailer space in fourteen countries. Pricing, operations and growth each had their own “on-time delivery” number, and each lived in a different analyst’s head.

02What they connected

It answered. Then it disagreed with the dashboard.

A ClickHouse cluster with 9.8 billion events, plus billing. Tomás Reyes booked the walkthrough to prove it would not work on Pallet’s schema. It answered the sample question, late deliveries by lane, with the SQL attached. Then he spent an hour finding where it disagreed with the dashboard. It was right in two of the three places.

03What changed

The slow questions got faster too.

Questions stopped waiting for a slot. The ones that still need an analyst, about one in twenty, now arrive with the SQL already drafted and the ambiguity already named.

04The number

From 212 to 9.

Questions that waited more than a week: 212 a quarter before, 9 after.

(03)Meal-kit subscriptions · Berlin · 520 people

Marrow

Churn spikes are noticed in two days, not twenty-one.

Marrow / Days from a churn spike to someone noticingBeforeAfter

Source: Marrow growth team, five tracked segments, spikes above an eight-week range.

01The question

The review met on Mondays. The churn did not.

Marrow ships 1.4 million boxes a month. When cancellations ticked up, the weekly churn review caught it about three weeks late, by which time the cause had usually changed.

02What they connected

One standing question, every morning at seven.

Subscriptions, support tickets and delivery events. The growth team saved one standing question: which segments are cancelling above their eight-week range? It runs at 07:00 and posts to #growth.

03What changed

Hamburg, in four days.

Spikes arrive with a segment, a likely cause and the query. In March a delivery-slot change in Hamburg pushed cancellations up 1.9 points. It was reversed in four days instead of three weeks.

04The number

Twenty-one days became two.

Across five segments, the time from spike to someone noticing fell from 21 days to 2.

(04)Neobank · Oslo · 900 people

Brevik

The security review took nine days, not eleven weeks.

Brevik / Security review to productionBeforeAfter

Source: Brevik security programme office. Previous tool reviewed in the prior year.

01The question

Eleven weeks, four hand-offs.

A bank’s analytics tool needs a security review. Brevik’s last one took eleven weeks: a questionnaire, a penetration-test review, legal, and a pilot approval nobody could find the owner of.

02What they connected

Verified in the first hour.

Enterprise, in a single-tenant Frankfurt region, with customer-managed keys and SCIM from their directory. The read-only grants were verified in the first hour. Sightline refused to connect once, because one role was wider than the ticket said.

03What changed

One meeting, with the diagram open.

The review became one meeting, with the audit log and the data-flow diagram open on screen. The questionnaire was answered by a person in two days; the DPA and sign-off followed within the week.

04The number

Nine days, door to door.

Nine days from first call to production, and the first analyst question was answered that afternoon.
