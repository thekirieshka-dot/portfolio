(01)StudioCopenhagen, since 2014

Linea is an architecture and interiors studio in Copenhagen. Eighteen people drawing houses, public buildings and rooms in Denmark and Sweden. We work with few materials, plaster, lime, oak and brick, and we draw the daylight before we draw the walls.

(a)People18

At one long table in a courtyard workshop in Christianshavn.

(b)Built64

Projects completed since 2014, from a bathroom to a town hall.

(c)Public9

Public buildings. Libraries, a reading room, a municipal hall, one arcade.

(d)Reach2

Countries. Most of our sites are within two hours of the studio.

Founded by Ida Thorsen and Mats Lindqvist, who met on a building site in Malmö and never quite left it.

(02)The archDrawing D-01

A wall that opens without a lintel.

The arch carries the wall around the opening and lets light fall deep into the room. We use it in brick and in plaster, at the scale of a niche and of a public hall.

It is the one shape we allow ourselves to repeat.

(04)PrinciplesDrawing D-02, ground floor
01

Daylight first.

We place the windows before the walls. Every room gets light from two sides or from above, and the plan follows the sun from the kitchen in the morning to the long table in the evening.

Windows and light on the plan
02

Few materials.

Brick, lime, plaster, oak. Fewer junctions, fewer things that fail. We would rather spend the budget on the thickness of a wall than on what covers it.

Load-bearing brick, lime-plastered
03

The arch, again.

A round opening is the oldest way to carry a wall over a void. We use it where rooms meet, so that passing from one to the next feels like a small event.

Arch over, see D-01
04

Rooms before objects.

We draw the table before the room around it. Furniture is planned at 1:20 with the walls, not chosen at the end. A good room tells you where to sit.

Table, bench, counter, labels
