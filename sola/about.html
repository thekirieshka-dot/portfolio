Our story

Made slowly, in good light.

SOLA began as a question at a kitchen table in Santos: why does everything made for sun-tired skin smell of something else?

In 2021 Inês Valadas, a cosmetic chemist from Setúbal, and Rui Cabral, who had spent three summers running a beach hut on the Alentejo coast, filled forty amber bottles by hand and gave them to the people who worked outside all day. Most came back empty within a month. Several came back with notes.

  1. 2021Forty bottles, one kitchen table, no fragrance.
  2. 2022Four formulas, after thirty-one test batches.
  3. 2023The refill programme starts. The first empties come home.
  4. 2026Batch 07, and 1,840 bottles refilled so far.

(02)The refill programme

The bottle comes home.

  1. Use it down to the last drop. Rinse with warm water and keep the pump or dropper: they go back with it.

  2. Every order arrives with a prepaid label folded into the lid of the box. Five empties fit; the post office does the rest.

  3. Each bottle is washed, then steamed at 121 °C for twenty minutes. Droppers and pumps are replaced every second trip.

  4. Filled by hand in batches of 120, given a new batch number, and sent back to you for 15% less than a new bottle.

(03)Where it comes from

Eleven origins, one table in Lisbon.

Hover a line below 
  1. 01

    Squalane

    Alentejo, Portugal38.0°N 7.9°W

    From olives pressed after the harvest, when the fruit is cheapest to the grove and best to us. Refined in Setúbal, forty minutes from the studio.

  2. 02

    Jojoba

    Souss valley, Morocco30.4°N 9.6°W

    Cold-pressed from one cooperative’s seed. A liquid wax rather than an oil, which is why it does not go rancid on a bathroom shelf.

  3. 03

    Niacinamide

    Shizuoka, Japan34.9°N 138.4°E

    Cosmetic-grade vitamin B3 from a maker that publishes its impurity data, which is rarer than it should be.

  4. 04

    Bakuchiol

    Madhya Pradesh, India23.5°N 78.0°E

    Extracted from babchi seed, harvested after the monsoon. Bought by the kilo, tested for purity by the gram.

  5. 05

    Rosehip

    Araucanía, Chile38.7°S 72.6°W

    Wild-gathered hips, cold-pressed within a week of picking. The oil comes out the colour of amber.

  6. 06

    Shea butter

    Northern Region, Ghana9.4°N 0.8°W

    Unrefined, from a women’s cooperative outside Tamale. They crush and boil by hand; we only filter it.

  7. 07

    Kaolin

    Aveiro, Portugal40.6°N 8.6°W

    White clay from a quarry two hours north of the studio. It is the reason Clay Barrier Cream reads satin, never greasy.

  8. 08

    Candelilla wax

    Coahuila, Mexico27.0°N 101.4°W

    Scraped from a desert shrub and boiled down in the field. The plant wax that keeps the balm solid through August.

  9. 09

    Ceramide NP

    Kyoto, Japan35.0°N 135.8°E

    Made by fermentation, not taken from animals. The same molecule skin builds its own barrier from.

  10. 10

    Amber glass

    Marinha Grande, Portugal39.7°N 8.9°W

    Blown in the town that has made Portuguese glass since 1769. Thick enough to be refilled thirty times.

  11. 11

    Linen cloth

    Guimarães, Portugal41.4°N 8.3°W

    Washable linen for the oil cleanse, woven and hemmed an hour north of Porto.
